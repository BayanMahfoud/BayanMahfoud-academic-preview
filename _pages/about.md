## About Me

I am a fourth-year undergraduate Artificial Intelligence student from Syria, currently building a research foundation in NeuroAI and computational neuroscience.

My long-term research interest is the study of consciousness and disorders of consciousness, with a particular interest in how computational models and adaptive neural interventions may help us understand—and eventually modulate—brain dynamics associated with conscious states.

I am currently at the literature-study and theory-development stage. I am studying neuroscience, consciousness research, neural dynamics, mathematical modelling, scientific Python, and reproducible research methods before defining a specific intervention mechanism.

This website documents my learning process, literature notes, computational experiments, research questions, and future work. I distinguish clearly between established evidence, working hypotheses, computational results, and clinical claims.

## Research Approach

I approach research through four stages:

1. Understanding the neuroscience and clinical problem.
2. Mapping existing theories, measurements, and interventions.
3. Translating testable hypotheses into mathematical and computational frameworks.
4. Evaluating proposed mechanisms through controlled, reproducible, and safety-aware experiments.

I am particularly interested in the gap between detecting residual or covert consciousness and developing interventions that can adapt to the changing dynamics of an individual brain.

At the current stage, my work is exploratory and theoretical. I do not present computational simulations as proof of clinical efficacy.
